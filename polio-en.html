Polio Vaccines

Polio vaccines are among the most important examples of how vaccination reshaped public health in the twentieth century.

Quick Research Card

AgentPoliovirus
Vaccine typesInactivated injected vaccine and live attenuated oral vaccine
Key figuresJonas Salk and Albert Sabin
Main effectDramatic reduction in paralytic polio

Medical Background

Polio can affect the nervous system and cause paralysis. Most infections do not cause paralysis, but the possibility of severe neurological injury made polio a major public fear.

Two Development Paths

Salk developed an inactivated injectable vaccine, while Sabin developed a live attenuated oral vaccine. Each played an important role in mass immunization strategies.

Etymology

Poliomyelitis comes from Greek: polios = gray, myelos = spinal cord, and -itis = inflammation.

Key point: polio shows how vaccination can turn social fear into global prevention.
