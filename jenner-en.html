Edward Jenner · 1749–1823

Edward Jenner

Edward Jenner is widely regarded as the father of modern vaccination because of his work on smallpox. He did not invent every form of immunization; variolation existed before him.

Historical Background

Smallpox was among the deadliest diseases in human history. Before Jenner, variolation exposed people to smallpox material in a controlled way, but the practice could still cause disease and transmission.

Jenner’s Contribution

Jenner observed the relationship between cowpox and protection from smallpox. His 1796 experiment became a symbol of modern vaccination because it offered a safer route to immune protection.
