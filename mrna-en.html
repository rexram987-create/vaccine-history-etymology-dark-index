mRNA Vaccines

mRNA vaccines became widely known during COVID-19, but they are based on decades of research in molecular biology, immunology, and nanoparticle delivery.

Basic Idea

Instead of injecting a whole virus or a prepared protein, an mRNA vaccine provides cells with a temporary instruction to make a specific protein. The immune system then learns to recognize that protein.

What is mRNA?

mRNA stands for messenger RNA. It carries information used by cellular machinery to make proteins. In vaccines, the instruction is temporary and does not integrate into human DNA.

Etymology

RNA means ribonucleic acid. The letter m stands for messenger, emphasizing the molecule’s role as a temporary biological message.

Key point: mRNA does not change DNA; it acts as a temporary instruction.
