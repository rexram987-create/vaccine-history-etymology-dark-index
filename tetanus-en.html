Tetanus Vaccine

The tetanus vaccine protects against a powerful neurotoxin produced by Clostridium tetani, and it shows why even a non-contagious disease can require routine vaccination.

Quick Research Card

AgentClostridium tetani
Vaccine typeToxoid
Typical entryContaminated wound and environmental spores
Public-health featureNo meaningful herd immunity

Medical Background

Tetanus can develop when spores enter damaged tissue under suitable conditions. The toxin affects nerve control and can cause spasms, muscle rigidity, difficulty swallowing, and lockjaw.

Why It Is Different

Tetanus usually does not spread from person to person. The risk is environmental, so community vaccination does not directly protect an unvaccinated individual from exposure through a wound.

Etymology

Tetanus comes from Greek tetanos, meaning tension or rigidity, a direct description of the disease’s characteristic muscle stiffness.

Key point: in tetanus, protection is personal; herd immunity cannot be relied upon.
