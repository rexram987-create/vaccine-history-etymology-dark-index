Louis Pasteur · 1822–1895

Louis Pasteur

Louis Pasteur did not invent vaccination, but he was one of the most important figures in transforming vaccination into a laboratory-based scientific method grounded in microbiology.

Identity Card

Life dates1822–1895
BirthplaceDole, France
Place of deathMarnes-la-Coquette, France
Main fieldMicrobiology, attenuated vaccines, rabies

His Place in Vaccine History

Pasteur worked almost a century after Jenner. His contribution was not the invention of vaccination itself, but the creation of a scientific framework for understanding disease agents, weakening them, and developing protective preparations.

Major Vaccine Work

Pasteur and his collaborators worked on experimental vaccines against chicken cholera, anthrax, and rabies. The 1885 rabies work became a landmark because it showed that intervention after exposure could prevent a fatal disease before symptoms appeared.

How He Differs from Jenner

Jenner worked from clinical observation and the relationship between cowpox and smallpox. Pasteur worked within a laboratory world of microorganisms, controlled attenuation, and systematic experimentation.
