Timeline

Historical timeline

Before 1796

Variolation

Controlled exposure to material from smallpox preceded modern vaccination, but it was riskier than later vaccine methods.

1796

Jenner and modern vaccination

Edward Jenner developed the smallpox vaccine. The term vaccine comes from Latin vacca, meaning cow.

1881

Pasteur and anthrax

Louis Pasteur helped transform vaccination into a laboratory-based scientific method using attenuation.

1885

Pasteur and rabies

Pasteur did not invent vaccination, but his rabies work became a landmark in laboratory vaccinology.

1894

Calmette and antivenom

Albert Calmette, of the Pasteur Institute tradition, developed early antivenom serum against snakebite.

1950s

Polio vaccines

Salk and Sabin developed major polio vaccines that reshaped public health.

2020

mRNA vaccines

mRNA vaccines became widely known through COVID-19 vaccination campaigns.

Etymology

Core terms

Vaccine

From Latin vacca, meaning cow, through Jenner’s work with cowpox.

Immunity

From Latin immunitas, meaning exemption or protection.

Variolation

From variola, smallpox; an older practice preceding vaccination.

Serum

The antibody-containing fluid part of blood after clotting; central to antivenom therapy.
