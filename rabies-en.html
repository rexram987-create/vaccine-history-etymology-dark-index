Rabies Vaccine

The rabies vaccine became a landmark of nineteenth-century microbiology because it showed that laboratory science could save lives even after exposure to a deadly disease.

Quick Research Card

Disease agentRabies lyssavirus
Main routeBite or saliva from an infected animal
Key figureLouis Pasteur
Medical uniquenessPost-exposure prevention

Medical Background

Rabies is a viral disease of the nervous system. Once symptoms appear, it is almost always fatal, so prevention after exposure is medically critical.

The Clinical Problem

The key challenge is the interval between exposure and symptoms. During this period, wound cleaning, vaccination, and sometimes rabies immune globulin can prevent disease.

Pasteur’s Turning Point

Pasteur and his collaborators developed a preventive approach after suspected exposure. This made the laboratory a central institution in modern preventive medicine.

Etymology

Rabies is linked to a Latin root associated with rage or madness, reflecting the severe neurological symptoms historically associated with the disease.

Public Health Importance

Rabies vaccination connects human medicine, veterinary medicine, travel medicine, and public health. It is a classic example of the One Health approach.

Key point: rabies shows the importance of post-exposure prevention.
